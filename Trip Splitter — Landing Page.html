How to install

This app isn't on the Play Store yet, so your phone will show a warning before installing. Here's exactly what to do — and why it happens.

1

Tap "Download APK" above

The file downloads straight to your phone — no app store needed.

2

Open the downloaded file

Your phone will show a warning like "Install blocked" or "For your security, your phone isn't allowed to install unknown apps from this source."

3

Tap "Settings" on that warning

It'll take you to a toggle for the app you used to download the file (e.g. Chrome, Files). Turn on "Allow from this source."

4

Go back and tap Install

That's it — the app installs normally, just like any other app.

Why does this warning show up at all?

This isn't something specific to Trip Splitter — Android shows this exact warning for every single app that isn't downloaded through the Play Store, regardless of who made it or how safe it is. It's a built-in Android safety feature, not a judgment on this app. Once you allow it for the source you downloaded from, you won't see it again for future updates from that same source.

Is it safe? Being fully honest

🔒

Your trip data stays on your phone

Trips, members, and expenses are stored locally on your device only. There's no server, no account, and no login — nothing about your trips is ever uploaded anywhere.

📶

Core features need zero internet

Adding trips, expenses, and calculating who-owes-who works completely offline. You can use the whole app in airplane mode.

📢

Ads only when you're online

This app shows ads through Google AdMob — the same ad service used by a huge share of Android apps. Ads only load when you have an internet connection; if you're offline, you simply won't see any. Like any ad network, AdMob handles its own advertising data per Google's policies — this is separate from your trip/expense data, which never leaves your device.

🧑‍💻

Built independently, not by a big company

This is a solo/independent project, not a funded startup or corporation. No hidden tracking, no data reselling — there's simply no infrastructure here to do that even if someone wanted to.
